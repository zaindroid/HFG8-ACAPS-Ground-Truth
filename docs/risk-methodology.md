# ACAPS Risk Methodology

> A forward-looking approach to identifying and prioritising potential humanitarian deteriorations.

For the full visual overview, see the [methodology infographic](../references/acaps-risk-methodology-infographic.png) and the [detailed methodology PDF](../references/acaps-risk-methodology.pdf).

---

## Core Concept

> **Risk = Probability x Impact**

Risk is the combination of the **probability** that a hazard will materialise and the **expected impact** if it does.

- **Impact** considers expected exposure, hazard intensity, people's vulnerability, and their coping capacity or resilience.
- **Probability** estimates the likelihood of the hazard occurring within a defined period.

---

## Six-Step Analytical Process

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Monitor** | Continuously monitor the situation, analyse context |
| **2. Identify** | Identify events that could lead to deterioration |
| **3. Assess** | Assess impact and probability |
| **4. Prioritise** | Determine overall risk level |
| **5. Disseminate** | Communicate findings to decision-makers |
| **6. Review** | Monitor developments, update assessments |

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Hazard** | A potential event or development that could cause a humanitarian deterioration |
| **Probability** | The likelihood that a hazard materialises (scored 1-5 from Very Low to Very High) |
| **Impact** | The expected humanitarian consequences if the hazard materialises (scored 1-5) |
| **Exposure** | The number of people who may require additional humanitarian assistance |
| **Vulnerability / Coping capacity** | Factors that increase or decrease the impact, including existing fragilities and capacity to absorb shocks |

---

## Identifying and Prioritising Risks

Risk identification draws on continuous monitoring of quantitative data and qualitative reporting across relevant countries and themes. Sources include ACAPS products, UN and government data, conflict monitoring, health and nutrition surveillance, climate and hazard data, media, and social media.

Analysts triangulate sources to identify emerging patterns and risks. Not every monitored issue becomes a featured risk — analysts use professional judgement and peer review to determine what warrants inclusion.

---

## Scoring, Uncertainty, and Outputs

Probability and impact are each assessed on a **1-5 scale** and combined to determine an overall risk level:

|  | Impact 1 | Impact 2 | Impact 3 | Impact 4 | Impact 5 |
|--|----------|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Probability: Very High** | Medium | Medium | High | High | High |
| **Probability: High** | Low | Medium | Medium | High | High |
| **Probability: Medium** | Low | Medium | Medium | Medium | High |
| **Probability: Low** | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| **Probability: Very Low** | Low | Low | Low | Low | Medium |

- **Scores reflect expert judgement**, not statistical precision
- **Uncertainty is acknowledged** — gaps should be explicit, and assessments updated as new information emerges

## Risk Types

The ACAPS Risk Radar tracks three types of risk:

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **New emerging risk** | A new hazard that could trigger a humanitarian crisis where none currently exists |
| **Marked deterioration in an existing crisis** | A development that could significantly worsen an ongoing humanitarian situation |
| **Future impact of a hazard which has already taken place** | Delayed or cascading consequences of an event that has already occurred |

---

## Risk Status

Each risk on the radar has a status that tracks what happened:

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Active** | The risk is still being monitored — the hazard has not yet fully materialised or been resolved |
| **Materialised** | The anticipated hazard or impact has occurred |
| **Not materialised** | The monitoring period ended without the risk materialising |

---

## Likely Humanitarian Impacts

The methodology considers these categories of humanitarian impact:

- **Displacement** — forced movement of populations
- **Reduced access to basic services** — healthcare, education, water
- **Market disruption** — supply chains, food prices, livelihoods
- **Protection concerns** — violence, exploitation, rights violations
- **Compounding crises** — cascading or overlapping emergencies
