# ACAPS personas mapped to Sealed Signals

Source: "Understanding our readers through personas", ACAPS (30 semi-structured phone interviews; two axes: geographical
coverage global/field and function information users/producers). Shared by the organisers on Saturday 7 November. The
organisers note the personas are also the kind of experts ACAPS engages with. Everything in the "assumed" columns is a
design hypothesis to verify in the pilot, not evidence.

## Emily — head of methodology, emergency response team, donor agency, HQ (global, producer)

- Her words: "at least with ACAPS I know what you don't know and what is been assumed"; "determining reliability in the
  information going into the analysis can be tricky"; wants "a more synthesised figure of how bad the humanitarian
  situation is", "a better analysis of the original data", "online access to all resources used to build an analysis
  even those that have different perspectives and the ones that are not online".
- What she gets: the ledger and honesty box (what fed in, what is unknown, misses), source tags saw/read/heard, the
  verification queue status (corroborated / single-source / uncorroborated), the Verify click.
- As an expert: panel member on health and nutrition themes across countries. Contact type: HQ methodologist.
  Assumed channel: email with one-click links; Teams where her agency permits. Assumed cadence: fine with monthly.
- As a reviewer: she is the person who will ask what "sealed" means. Section 1a answers her.

## Jose — emergency specialist, UN regional office, Jordan; covers Iraq, Sudan, Djibouti, Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria (regional, user and producer)

- His words: wants "an easy way for field-users to provide quality feedback to HQ level in order to get a more accurate
  picture of the humanitarian situation"; sees "discrepancies between information shared and the real situation"; wants
  "email updates only for the countries I cover"; uses ACAPS app alerts; "sometimes the data I get is different from my
  colleagues, which triggers interesting discussions and pushes the analysis forward".
- What he gets: the pulse itself. He covers Sudan, so he is on the R1043 panel in the demo story. Alerts filtered to
  his countries. The feedback message after the reveal, which is the "interesting discussion" he describes, structured.
- Contact type: regional desk expert. Assumed channel: email with one-click links, Teams; app notification for alerts.
  Assumed cadence: weekly is acceptable for the countries he covers.
- Design consequence: pulses and alerts are filtered by coverage area, never broadcast.

## Pierre — PMER / MEAL officer, IFRC, deployed for months; food crisis, Nairobi (field, producer)

- His words: "when I start in a new mission I always try to find the most reliable sources"; values "in-depth reports
  for a specific context"; "our resources are limited and we don't have time to produce overview situation"; wants
  scenario-planning skills and "the right habits".
- What he gets: as a reader, the trigger list and the question contract per risk (a concrete scenario frame). As an
  expert, the one-screen card with "outside my area" as a real answer, since he is deployed across contexts; voice
  notes because he is in the field.
- Contact type: field practitioner. Assumed channel: WhatsApp, voice note, scheduled call. Assumed cadence: pauses
  during deployments; "pause" by one word matters for him.
- Design consequence: relayed answers through a liaison exist for the local staff he works with, labelled as such.

## Aisha — country director, INGO, Madagascar, one country, 150 staff (field, user)

- Her words: "my financial visibility rarely goes beyond 6–10 months"; "what I don't get is someone telling me how the
  next presidential elections will affect my programming"; wants "an alerts system" and "political projections".
- What she gets: the ledger's "what decision could this inform, for whom, by when" line, written for exactly her
  decision (keep an emergency team or not). The six-month question horizon matches her planning horizon.
- As an expert: one country, rarely available; a panel member for Madagascar only. Contact type: country lead.
  Assumed channel: email weekly digest, scheduled call. Assumed cadence: low; never more than one pulse a month.
- Design consequence: the decision line on the ledger is mandatory, not optional.

## Where each persona appears in the demo

0:40 Jose's phone receives the Chad pulse (email, WhatsApp, SMS leaving the Reach module). 2:30 Emily reads the honesty
box and clicks Verify. 2:45 Aisha reads the decision line. Pierre is the "outside my area" answer in the jury exercise.

## What the personas do not establish

Channel preference, willingness to be calibrated, response rate, tolerance for a weekly cadence. Only the pilot does.
