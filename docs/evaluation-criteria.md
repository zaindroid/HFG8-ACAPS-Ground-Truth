# Evaluation Criteria

## What You Deliver

At the end of the hackathon, each team delivers:

1. **A working prototype or demo** — it does not need to be production-ready, but it should demonstrate your core idea clearly.
2. **A 3-minute pitch for the jury** — concise, focused, compelling.
3. **A repository link and a short description** — what you built, how it works, and why.

---

## MoSCoW Criteria

Solutions are assessed using the **MoSCoW method** — must, should, should not, could, can have:

### Must Have

- **Easy to use** — feel like an interesting community and experience that experts want to come back to
- **Expert-controlled anonymity** — each expert chooses their own level (e.g., known to ACAPS, pseudonymous to peers, or fully anonymous)
- **Public anonymity** — individual contributions are always anonymous to the public

### Should Have

- **Sustained engagement** — maintain deep engagement over time while respecting experts' time
- **Short, focused contributions** — minutes, not long sessions
- **Small trusted groups** — fit a known, trusted group moderated by ACAPS, deliberately kept small (5-10 experts per country/context)

### Should Not

- **Engagement metrics as the goal** — this is about quality of information, not time spent in the app
- **AI chatbot feel** — the experience should feel human, not like talking to a bot

### Could Have

- **Super estimator scoreboard** — recognise consistently accurate experts over time
- **Agreement/disagreement visualisation** — show where experts agree and disagree, including by geography
- **Mobile and low-bandwidth support** — many experts work in the field
- **Multilingual support** — French and other working languages of field experts

### Can Have

- **AI behind the scenes** — help ACAPS moderators synthesise expert input, as long as the expert-facing experience stays human

## Judging Dimensions

While specific jury weights may vary, strong solutions typically excel across these dimensions:

| Dimension | What the Jury Looks For |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Problem understanding** | Does the team grasp the real challenge ACAPS faces? |
| **Expert experience** | Would a busy humanitarian expert actually use this? |
| **Anonymity & trust** | Is the anonymity model thoughtful and robust? |
| **Feasibility** | Could this realistically be piloted with real experts? |
| **Data use** | Does the solution meaningfully use the Risk Radar data? |
| **Innovation** | Does the approach bring something new to expert consultation or forecasting? |
| **Presentation** | Is the 3-minute pitch clear, compelling, and well-structured? |

---

## Follow-Up Opportunity

The **2-3 most promising solutions** will be tested in a pilot with a small ACAPS expert group. ACAPS is happy to host a presentation where teams discuss their solution with the ACAPS team.

The pilot can start **right after the hackathon or in early December 2026**, running for approximately two months.

> Solutions that make joining and staying involved easy have a clear head start for the pilot.

---

## Tips for Strong Submissions

- **Design for the expert, not the analyst.** You are building for ACAPS, but the user is the expert in the field.
- **Respect experts' time.** If it takes more than a few minutes to contribute, it is too much.
- **Use the data to validate.** The Risk Radar export has real outcomes — use them to demonstrate that your approach works.
- **Show the loop.** The strongest solutions show how expert input feeds back into ACAPS analysis and how outcomes are shared publicly.
- **Think about retention.** Expert communities tend to lose participation after launch. How do you prevent that?
- **Build your demo around R1043** — the Chad/Sudan risk is a live, active, high-rated risk with rich data.
